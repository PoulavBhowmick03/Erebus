# Mainnet 0.6/0.4 canary — 2026-08-31

The second complete bounded Erebus workflow on Starknet mainnet, run the same day as
[the first](./2026-08-31-mainnet-starkscan-workflow.md). It settled a different split from a
different negotiation path: the buyer opened below the seller's reserve, the seller countered
up, and the buyer accepted. The first canary matched on the opening offer and never conceded.

This run was driven **by an MCP client over the tool surface**, one tool call at a time,
rather than by the scripted policy loop. No local transaction-prover or screening interceptor
ran.

## MCP negotiation and conservation

Two packaged MCP servers ran against the seam backend, one per identity, role-bound to payer
and payee. Each of the thirteen tools was exposed; the negotiation used `doctor`,
`get_note_balance`, `read_channel_state`, `propose_offer`, `counter_offer`,
`accept_and_settle`, `reconcile`, `grant_viewing_key`, and `reveal`.

The buyer read its balance, opened at 0.48 STRK, and the seller read that offer through its
reverse channel view. Because 0.48 was below its 0.6 reserve, the seller countered at the
reserve rather than matching. The buyer re-read the channel, found the 0.6 counter within
budget, and accepted the seller-authored offer.

The settlement returned:

- Selected input: 1 STRK — the shielded note alone.
- Paid: 0.6 STRK. Change: 0.4 STRK.
- Conservation: `1.0 = 0.6 + 0.4` STRK.
- Spent nullifier: `0x70af73ec86b8f0504b41d74583b8be15b0acec2a59de940b9a8d3e85eb50224`.
- Proved at block 14147348, submitted and accepted in 14147370.

Post-settlement note discovery returned:

- Account A: 0.4 STRK change plus the 0.2 STRK note left over from the earlier canary.
- Account B: 0.6 STRK payment plus its 0.8 STRK note from the earlier canary.

Input selection chose the minimal-surplus subset: it spent the single 1 STRK note and left
A's unrelated 0.2 STRK note untouched, rather than consuming both and returning 0.6.

## Recovery, observer, and disclosure

`reconcile` on both identities classified every operation in the journal — including the four
from the earlier canary — as `outcome: effect`, `stage: committed`, `next_action: none`, with
chain effect, local state, and result in agreement. No write was retried and no replacement
operation ID was minted.

The public observer inspected the settlement's 61 calldata felts and 8 candidate note salts
with no channel key, pool key, local state, or grant. It recovered no plausible transcript and
did not match the wire-v2 fixed fifth-salt shape. This does not hide the submitting accounts,
the channel relationship, timing, action shape, or note count.

Account A then created a recipient-bound grant for deal `10977364695535158093`, scoped to
Account B with a one-hour expiry. B opened it from a mode-0600 file and reconstructed exactly
the three offers of that deal plus its settlement, with `agreed_amount` and `paid_amount` both
0.6 STRK. Their equality makes the disclosed settlement consistent; the reveal document does
not contain a separate `consistency` field. The earlier 0.8 STRK deal in the same channel did
not appear in the reveal output, which is the deal-scoping working as designed. Grant creation
and reveal are local operations; they have no transaction hash and confer no spending authority.

## Result

The bounded mainnet workflow passed a second time, on a negotiation path the first canary did
not exercise: a real concession from 0.48 to 0.6, and a minimal-surplus note selection that
left an unrelated note alone. Screened shield, note discovery, MCP negotiation, atomic payment
and change, reconciliation, public-observer negative control, and recipient-bound selective
disclosure all held.

This remains two bounded canaries between two operator-controlled identities. It is not
evidence of audit, capacity, uptime, independent review, or safe handling of material value.
